We've all heard 1 Corinthians 13 at weddings. It's beautiful, it's familiar, and it's easy to let it stay beautiful and familiar.

But read it as a list of verbs and it becomes one of the most demanding passages in Scripture.

## Love is not a feeling, it's a practice

Patient. Kind. Not envious, not boastful, not proud. Paul isn't describing a mood — he's describing a muscle. Every one of these is something you *do*, often before you feel it.

Love that doesn't keep score is love that refuses to remember who owes whom.

## The scoreboard comes down

Storing up offenses is exhausting. It turns relationships into ledgers. And the ledger always wins — because someone always owes.

Jesus tore up the ledger. The cross is God saying, "I'm not keeping score anymore." And we get to live like that's true.

## What love is not

Paul tells us what love isn't: easily angered, record-keeping, delighting in evil. Then he tells us what it is: it always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres.

This week, ask one question before you speak: *Is this patient? Is this kind?* The answer changes everything.
