Paul ends his love chapter with a strange kind of comfort: everything impressive about the Christian life is temporary.

Prophecies stop. Tongues fall silent. Knowledge becomes obsolete. The gifts that made the Corinthians proud were always going to expire.

## What lasts

But love never fails. Not because love is the most impressive thing, but because love is the most *real* thing. It's the only currency that survives the transition from this age to the next.

We build our lives out of so many things that won't survive the move. Love is the one thing we can take with us.

## Through a glass, dimly

Paul admits what we all suspect: we don't see clearly yet. We see a reflection, a riddle, a partial picture. One day we'll see face to face — but that day isn't today.

Today, we love in the everyday. We practice in the traffic, the kitchen table, the checkout line.

## The greatest of these

Faith points at what we trust. Hope points at what's coming. Love points at who God is — because God is love.

The everyday is the gymnasium where eternal people are formed. Love well today, and you'll be ready for forever.
