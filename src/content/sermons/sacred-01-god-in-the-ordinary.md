The kingdom of God is like a man who scatters seed on the ground, then goes to bed. Night and day the seed sprouts and grows — though he doesn't know how.

It's the least dramatic parable Jesus ever told, and maybe the most reassuring.

## The quiet work of God

Most of our lives are not mountaintop moments. They're seed-and-sleep seasons: showing up, doing the next right thing, trusting that something is growing under the surface.

The farmer doesn't understand the biology. He just trusts the process. The kingdom grows while we're not looking.

## Sacredness is hiding in plain sight

We keep waiting for God to show up in the spectacular. Jesus keeps pointing to the ordinary: seed, soil, bread, water, a neighbor, a Tuesday.

The holy is not somewhere else. It's here, disguised as the everyday — if we have eyes to see it.

## The harvest comes

The parable ends with the grain ripening and the man putting the sickle to it. Growth takes time, but harvest is certain.

Whatever you're sowing today — in a child, a marriage, a calling, a hard season — don't despise the ordinary days. God is doing His quiet, faithful work.
