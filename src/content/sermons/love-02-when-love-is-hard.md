We know this story. The younger son demands his inheritance, wastes it, and comes home to a father who runs to meet him. It's the most famous picture of grace in the Bible.

But Jesus didn't end the story there. He ended it with the older brother standing outside the party, furious.

## The older brother's math

The older son did everything right. Stayed home. Worked. Obeyed. And he's the one standing in the dark, because his obedience had become a transaction.

He loved the father's *stuff*, not the father. When grace showed up, it didn't fit his math — so he refused to come in.

## The father goes out to both

Here's the detail that should wreck us: the father leaves the party twice. Once to run to the younger son, and once to plead with the older.

There is no version of you that the Father won't walk toward.

## Love is hard when it's undeserved

Loving the person who hurt you, the sibling who got everything, the one who never apologized — that's the love that only works when we've first received it ourselves.

The party is going on inside. The question is whether we'll let grace be bigger than our math.
