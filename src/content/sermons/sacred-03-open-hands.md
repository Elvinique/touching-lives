"Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth." It's one of the most counter-cultural sentences Jesus ever spoke — and also one of the most practical.

Because Jesus isn't mainly talking about money. He's talking about anxiety.

## Clenched fists and worried hearts

The things we hoard are usually the things we don't trust God with. We hold tighter to what we're afraid to lose. Clenched fists are the posture of fear; open hands are the posture of faith.

Jesus names it plainly: you can't serve both God and money. One of them will be your master.

## Treasure maps the heart

Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also. It's a diagnostic, not a command to be poor. It's an invitation to notice: what am I actually investing in?

The heart follows whatever we spend our lives on. Invest in the things that survive the grave.

## The freedom of open hands

Giving isn't God's way of getting our money. It's God's way of unclenching our fists — so we can receive the one treasure that moths and rust can't touch.

You can't out-give God. Open your hands and see what happens.
