A man threw a banquet and sent his servant to call the guests. One by one they made their excuses — a field to inspect, oxen to try, a new marriage. All reasonable. All disappointing.

Jesus tells this story to a room full of people who were comfortable being invited but never quite showing up.

## Excuses are just well-dressed priorities

The guests didn't say no. They said *later*. They said *busy*. They said *I have other things first*.

Most of us aren't rejecting God — we're just scheduling Him around everything else. The tragedy of the parable is that the invitation didn't expire. The guests just never showed.

## The table keeps widening

When the invited guests didn't come, the master sent his servant to the streets and alleys — to the poor, the crippled, the blind, the lame. The people everyone assumed weren't invited turned out to be the guest list.

This is the heart of the gospel: the table was always meant to be full, and there is always room for one more.

## You are the invited guest

The invitation isn't for a perfect version of you. It's for the you that's tired of eating alone, the you that's been making excuses, the you that secretly wants a seat.

The table is set. Your chair is still there.
